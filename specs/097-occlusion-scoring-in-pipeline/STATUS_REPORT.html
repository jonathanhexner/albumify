Key mental model: the score, the box, and the mask are three different components with three different maturity levels. Your questions below each land on a different one.

1 · Your questions, answered directly

Q: Do we produce a blurry score?
Yes — two of them. (1) A per-image P(occluded) from the CLIP tile probe — this is the benchmarked number (0.83 scene PR-AUC, out-of-fold, i.e. every image scored by a model that never trained on its scene). Stored in model_scores.csv (B_clip_oof), browsable per image in the review app. (2) Per-tile scores (3×3) saying which region drives it. The red box you saw in the panels is a third, separate thing — a localization heuristic, not a score.
Q: Didn't we measure AUC? How can there be sky false alarms?
Because the AUC was measured for the detector, not for the box. The 0.83 PR-AUC belongs to the CLIP probe's per-image score. The red blur-box is a physics heuristic (LoG patch statistics) that we built for the localization research — it was validated only visually ("are the 2 real fingers boxed?"), never AUC-scored, and it runs unconditionally in the research panels — no detector gating. That's the gap. Details in §3.
Q: So how exactly did the sky false alarms happen?
The box rule looks for patches that are flat but noisy (defocused occluders kill detail but sit on a sensor-noise floor; sky is flat AND silent). The physics bands overlap: a dark finger and a hazy/textured sky both land at median-LoG ≈ 0.09–0.13× the image norm. No threshold separates them — we proved this in the spec-096 feature study (hand-crafted ceiling ≈ 0.5 PR-AUC for the same reason). Today's audit: 47 boxes on 122 images — 2/2 real fingers ✅, 45 false alarms, of which 24 are top-of-frame (sky/haze) and most of the rest are dark evening foregrounds. §3 shows both cases.
Q: And severity should depend on the main object — where does that stand?
Exactly — that is spec-097 Stage 2, and it is also why the sky FPs are tolerable by design: the penalty is overlap-driven, and sky never overlaps the subject. The subject-mask ladder (YOLO → SLIC-refined saliency fusion → heavy segmentation only if needed) is panel-validated as of today — §4 shows the masks. Nothing is implemented in the pipeline yet; it's specced and gated.

Two mitigations, one already designed, one planned: (1) Detector gating — the box is a where tool, consulted only when the learned whether (CLIP probe) fires. Un-gated panels overstate the problem: the 45 FPs mostly sit on images the probe scores clean. (2) Learned localization — the synthetic compositor produces pixel-exact occluder masks for free; a per-patch head trained on them replaces the physics thresholds entirely (spec-097 "durable localization" note). We deliberately did NOT tune thresholds further — the feature study showed physics cues plateau (~0.5 PR-AUC).

The severity formula (spec-097 Stage 2, agreed design)

penalty = w_subject · P(occluded) · overlap(blur_region, subject_mask)   ← photo-ruiner term
        + w_bg      · P(occluded) · area(blur_region − subject_mask)    ← cosmetic term

background-only blob → L1-ish   ·   subject partly covered → L2   ·   subject mostly covered → L3
driver is always the LEARNED P — never raw blur — so bokeh/portrait backgrounds are safe.
