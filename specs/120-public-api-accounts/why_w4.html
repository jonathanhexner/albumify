Why W4 exists

spec-120 · Accounts and a public API · 2026-11-28 · answering "was everything just idle?"

Nothing has been idle. No member of the public has ever uploaded a photo, so no public job has ever waited.

The upload code (W3) was written today and has only run inside tests. It isn't deployed and has no users. When I said "a completed upload just sits there," I meant that the code which picks it up hasn't been written yet. Nothing real is waiting: zero jobs, for zero minutes.

Your own albums never wait either. They start the moment you click Run, exactly as before.

How it works today, and what changes for the public

Today: your runs works, unchanged

You click Run in the Streamlit app on this PC
↓
The owner API (on this PC) makes the job row and starts it immediately (background.add_task(svc.run) in api/routers/jobs.py)
↓
Photos are read from your disk (D:\Slovakia26) and staged
↓
It runs here, or on the L4 GPU started from this PC; the worker writes results to Neon

There's no waiting room because there's only one user, the starter is your click, and your PC is on when you click.

Public site: a stranger's upload being built

A user uploads from the website at any hour (W3, built today)
↓
Their photos land in R2 cloud storage, not on your disk; the job row says queued
↓
W4: who presses "Run"? Not you, and not your PC, which may be off. A small dispatcher on Modal checks every 30 s: oldest first, at most 2 at once, within the $50 spend cap
↓
W4: a Modal CPU step shrinks the originals from R2 to 768 px (today your PC does this)
↓
The same GPU worker as today writes the album to Neon

Why your click can't just be copied

Your runsPublic uploadsSo W4 needs
Who starts itYou, by clickingNobody clicks; the upload just endsSomething that starts jobs by itself
Where it starts fromThis PC, which is onMust work with this PC offThe starter lives on Modal, not here
Where the photos areYour diskR2 (full-size originals)A step that shrinks them on Modal
How many at onceOne, yoursSeveral users at the same timeA queue: in order, at most 2 running, spend cap checked
If the API restartsYou click againA user's upload must not be lostThe queue lives in the database, not in memory

Why check every 30 s instead of starting the job the moment the upload finishes? Then a restart could never drop a job, and the "2 at a time" and spend-cap rules sit in one place. The cost is up to 30 s of delay on a run that takes 10–20 minutes.
