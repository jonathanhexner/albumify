Spec-022 · Geo-Temporal Album Enrichment

How we group photos by place & time — without assuming everyone is on a trip

The pipeline learns where, when, who, and what each photo is about, then lets several ways of grouping compete. The best-scoring one wins — and if none is convincing, it declines to segment rather than invent a story. The wording adapts so we never label a birthday party "Your trip to…".

The big idea: two separate jobs

The original spec blurred two very different jobs into one word ("trip"). We split them apart. The first job is pure geometry and makes no claim about meaning. The second job decides meaning.

Layer 1 is the same math for home photos and for Rome. Only Layer 2 decides what the group means.

The grouping job is a competition — and it can be declined

"Group by place & time" is only one way to organise an album. Some albums are really organised by who's in them, or by what's happening. So Layer 1 isn't a single algorithm — it runs several candidate axes in parallel, scores each one, and keeps the winner. Crucially, if the best score is still weak, it keeps nothing and shows a flat gallery. We never impose structure that isn't there.

Four candidate axes compete on one score. Winner-takes-all — or, below the floor, no segmentation at all.

The four axes

GEO · where

Cluster by GPS location (§1). Wins for travel and multi-place albums.

TIME · when

Cluster by capture-time gaps. Wins when there's no GPS but clear temporal bursts.

IDENTITY · who

Group by the people present, reusing the face-clustering people_clusters we already compute. Wins when one or two people are the theme — "Noa through the year" — and place/time are flat.

SEMANTIC · what

Group by scene content via cheap image captions (below). The flexible fallback when the other three are weak. Off by default — it's the most expensive and least deterministic.

The quality score — "is this segmentation real, or imposed?"

Every candidate axis is scored 0–1 from four cheap signals. The best axis wins; if even the best is below a floor, we decline to segment. This is the safety valve against forcing structure onto an album that doesn't have any.

ComponentAsksScore drops when…
Separationare groups far apart in the axis vs. their internal spread? (silhouette-style) groups bleed into each other — no real boundary
Coveragewhat fraction of photos even carry the needed signal? most photos lack GPS / time / faces for that axis
Balancesensible group sizes — not one blob, not N singletons one giant group + dust, or hundreds of fragments
Stabilitydoes it survive a small nudge of the threshold? a 30→35 km tweak reshuffles everything — fragile
The floor is the "drop it if we're strongly off" rule. Below it the album shows as a flat gallery (optionally with plain month headers) and makes no place/event/person claim. Silence beats a wrong story.

The semantic axis — cheap ViT captions, then the LLM

For the "what's happening" axis we run a small vision model that writes a one-line caption per image ("kids playing on a beach"). Those captions are tiny text, cached per photo just like embeddings — so the cost is one-time. The LLM then reasons over the text, never the pixels.

Two jobs, very different cost. Naming from a small sample is cheap and runs often; clustering all captions from scratch is a token blow-up, so it's reserved as the fallback axis.

Cost guardrail: we never hand the LLM all 1,000 captions to invent groupings by default — that's expensive and non-deterministic. The semantic axis mainly names segments the cheaper axes already found, and only acts as a clusterer when everything else fails.

1 · Grouping — the clustering algorithm

Every image carries (at best) a GPS coordinate and a capture time. Some carry only one; some carry neither. We branch on what's available, then cluster.

"30 km" and the time-gap threshold are config knobs (geo_radius_km, time_gap_hours).

This stage assumes nothing. A cluster of 40 photos near one coordinate over 3 hours could be a wedding, a park afternoon, or breakfast in Lisbon. Grouping just says "these belong together." Naming comes next.

2 · The fragmentation trap (kids/home photos)

Here's the failure you intuited. If you blindly split on every 8-hour gap, two years of everyday home photos shatter into hundreds of meaningless 8-hour "segments." That's noise, not structure.

❌ Naïve fixed split

~200 tiny "segments" over 2 years. Useless to browse.

✓ Context-adaptive granularity

Home life groups by month/season, not by burst.

Rule: granularity follows meaning. Once a segment is judged everyday / at-home, group it coarsely (month or season). A trip keeps the fine place+burst grouping, because there every day is different. So Layer 2 feeds back into Layer 1's cutting.

3 · The "home" anchor — the key to telling trips from routine

One simple idea does most of the work: home = the place where most of life happens. We compute it automatically per album as the GPS cluster holding the most distinct days. Then a "trip" is simply photos taken far from home; routine is photos near home.

Home is re-derived for every album, so it adapts to whoever's photos these are — no manual setup.

4 · The classifier — signals → "kind"

Once the GEO/TIME axis wins, each segment still needs a label. For each segment we already have cheap, structured signals (no model needed): distance from home, how many days it spans, how dense it is, how many distinct people appear (from face clustering). A small set of rules maps those to a kind, and the kind picks the caption template. (The IDENTITY and SEMANTIC axes carry their own labels — a person name, or a captioned theme.)

TRIP DAY-OUT EVENT EVERYDAY UNSORTED
KindSignal signatureCaption template (example)
TRIP far from home · spans multiple days or multiple places "A week in Dubai" · "Exploring the Austrian Alps"
DAY-OUT far from home · single day "A day at Lake Balaton"
EVENT any place · short burst · high photo density · many distinct faces "A lovely gathering, July 2024"
EVERYDAY at / near home · low density · spread over a long span "Everyday moments at home, spring 2024"
UNSORTED no usable GPS & no usable time (no place/time claim — plain "Untagged photos")

The word "trip" only ever appears for the TRIP kind. Home and event photos get their own warm, accurate wording.

How a single segment is decided

A compact rule tree. Each branch is a threshold on a signal we already compute. Every segment also gets a confidence from how clearly it fell on one side of each threshold.

5 · Where the LLM fits — narrow, local, optional

You asked whether to use an LLM to decide. My recommendation: not as the primary decider. The rules above settle the clear majority instantly, offline, and reproducibly. The LLM earns its keep in exactly two narrow spots — and only a local model, because GPS is private and the pipeline must not call the network.

(A) Tie-break the kind

Only for low-confidence segments. The model receives a compact JSON fact-sheet — {city, dist_from_home_km, n_days, n_photos, n_people, time_of_day} — never raw coordinates over a network, never the photos. It returns a kind. Most runs never trigger this.

(B) Phrase the caption

Turns the structured facts into one warm sentence. Default is a deterministic template; flip caption_strategy: local_llm to route through a small local model for richer wording. This is the flag we already stubbed in Slice 2.

Privacy & reproducibility guardrail: any LLM here is local-only (NFR-002: no external calls during the pipeline). Default behaviour with both flags off is 100% deterministic — same album in, same segments & captions out.

6 · Worked examples — same pipeline, three very different albums

Album A · "a year of travel" (the obvious case)

Two far-from-home multi-day blocks → TRIP. The rest is correctly everyday, grouped by month — not sliced into a hundred fragments.

Album B · "our kids this year" (your worry — no trips at all)

All near home → EVERYDAY grouped by season, with one dense high-people burst lifted out as an EVENT (the birthday). Zero "trips" invented.

Album C · "Maya's wedding weekend" (single place, single burst)

High density + many distinct faces, even if near-ish home → EVENT, not a trip. Caption: "A beautiful celebration, June 2024."

8 · Where it's stored (recap)

Per image

lat, lon, capture-time → columns on the existing per-image table image_metric_rows (one row / image / run). Queryable, joinable.

Per segment

the groupings (kind, caption, date range, city, member photos, confidence) → new geo_segments JSON column on pipeline_results (one blob / run).

Both are built from the single image_metrics dict in pipeline_service.py, so the JSON blob and the normalized tables can't drift apart.
