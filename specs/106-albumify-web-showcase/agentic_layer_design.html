The Agentic & Semantic Layer

Where AI reasoning and language actually belong on top of the curation engine — and, just as important, where they don't. The visual pipeline stays deterministic and cheap; a thin semantic layer and a few targeted AI decision points sit above it.

"Cheap deterministic engine does the heavy lifting; a VLM is called only where the choice is genuinely hard or genuinely linguistic — never per pixel."

01 · The shape

Three layers, one pipeline

The existing 28-step engine is a fixed dependency graph — the step order is forced and correct, so there is nothing for an LLM "orchestrator" to improve there (and re-deciding it each run would reintroduce the exact reorder bug spec-053 engineered out). Instead: leave the visual layer alone, add a cheap semantic layer, and escalate only the hard calls to a VLM.

Token discipline: the AI layer never sees all 800 images. It sees album-level summaries, a handful of escalated pairs, or the ~20 survivors. That is what keeps it pennies-per-run instead of a per-image bill.

02 · Two clustering jobs

"Multi-modal clustering" is really two different problems

The instinct to add text to clustering is right — but only for one of the two jobs. Fusing captions into the dedup clustering would make it worse. DINOv2 not "describing" the image isn't a flaw here: dedup needs "same pixels," not "same words."

Job A — have it

Scene dedup

"Are these the same moment? Collapse the near-identical."

  • Right modality: visual embeddings (DINOv2) + time/GPS.
  • Needs fine pixel-level distinctions — different faces, lighting, framing.
  • Adding captions here over-merges (see below).
→ keep single-modality, deterministic
Job B — new

Thematic grouping

"Show me all the food / all the architecture across the trip."

  • Right modality: text / semantic (CLIP tags, captions).
  • Cross-moment, cross-day — DINOv2 won't do this cleanly.
  • Powers browsing, narrative sections, album-type.
→ new semantic layer, cheap tags
Why text hurts dedup — the over-merge trap

Two genuinely different photos both caption to "a group of people at a dinner table" → text says "identical" → wrongly merged. DINOv2 sees different people/lighting/composition → correctly keeps them apart. And a sunset from Day 1 and a different sunset from Day 6 both read "a sunset over the water" — text would merge across days; visual + time keeps them separate. Text is coarser than pixels for "same moment."

03 · Two-tier captions

Caption the survivors richly, tag the roll cheaply

You're right that captions are useful regardless. The trick that keeps it affordable is when and with what you caption. Two tiers, matched to cost.

TIER 1 · ALL ~800
CLIP zero-shot tags against a fixed label set (food, portrait, landscape, architecture, night…). CLIP is already resident for occlusion scoring — no new model on the memory floor, no tokens.
near-free
TIER 2 · ~20 keepers
VLM free-text captions only on the selected album. Rich descriptions → story ordering, "why this photo" text, scene labels ("Day 3 — Buda Castle"), searchable text.
~20 calls · pennies

Captioning all 800 with a VLM is the "burning tokens" failure you were wary of. Captioning the survivors is 40× cheaper and is where free-text actually earns its keep. Tier 1 gives whole-roll semantics for the price of a matrix multiply you're already paying.

05 · AI as reference

A VLM as a yardstick — but not a teacher to copy

"Use AI as a reference our pipeline thrives toward" is a good idea with one sharp edge. Using a frontier VLM to measure and find our blind spots is high-value. Tuning our thresholds to maximize agreement with it is a trap.

Use it as

  • A reference peer. "Are our picks in the same ballpark as a frontier model?" — the honest head-to-head (spec-102).
  • A scalable judge. LLM-as-judge scores our picks when human raters aren't available — cheap iteration signal between human checkpoints.
  • A divergence finder. The most valuable output: the set where we and the VLM disagree. A human decides who's right — that set is where the pipeline actually improves.

Don't use it as

  • Ground truth. A human's blind pick is truth (spec-102 says so). VLMs have their own photo-selection quirks.
  • A distillation target. Tune to match the VLM and you just become a cheaper, worse VLM proxy — capped at its quality, inheriting its blind spots.
  • A circular benchmark. If a VLM is also inside the pipeline (arbiter), scoring against the same model measures agreement with yourself.
The hierarchy that keeps it honest

Human blind pick = north star (truth).  ·  VLM = strong reference peer + cheap judge for fast iteration.  ·  Our own objective metrics = the defensible numbers (Kendall tau vs human ranking, duplicate-survival rate, per-person coverage). Steer toward the human; use the VLM to move fast and surface disagreements between checkpoints.

07 · Test datasets

Where to get photo collections to test curation

The quality benchmarks you already have on disk (RealBlur, SIDD, PIQA23, AVA) measure single-image quality — not "did we pick the right photos from an album." For curation you need collections with a human-chosen subset or best-of. Two public datasets are built for exactly this.

Recommended split

PhotoTriage to tune/validate dedup + best-of-burst (its human "best" is a clean per-series label). CUFED to validate whole-album importance ranking against 5-vote human importance (report Kendall tau). Your trips + fresh Google Photos exports for the realistic end-to-end and the VLM A/B.
