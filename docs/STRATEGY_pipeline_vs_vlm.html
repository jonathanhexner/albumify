Albumify · strategy note · 2026-07-21

It isn't pipeline or VLM. They solve opposite-shaped problems.

The curation task splits cleanly into structure (global, combinatorial — the pipeline's home turf) and judgment (local, semantic — where a VLM earns its cost). The design question is where to put the seam, not which side to delete.

The reframe

"Best 20 photos of 800, organized by theme" is really two problems wearing one coat, and they have opposite computational shapes. Mixing them up is what makes the pipeline-vs-VLM debate feel unresolvable.

Structure · reduction

The pipeline wins

Global, O(N²), over all 800 photos at once.

  • De-dup & near-duplicate collapse
  • Group same scene (visual + your new time/geo fusion)
  • Group same person (face embeddings)
  • Segment by event / place (time backbone)

Deterministic, cheap, scales to thousands. A VLM cannot do this — it can't hold 800 photos in context or compare photo #3 to #700 reliably.

Judgment · selection

The VLM wins

Local, semantic, over a handful of candidates.

  • Best-of-N within one moment (5 near-dupes → the keeper)
  • "Is this worth keeping?" — interest, not just sharpness
  • Theme / caption / album arc (a language task)
  • Narrative ordering of the survivors

Aesthetic & semantic judgment, improving fast. Expensive per image — so only ever run it on the small set the pipeline already reduced to.

Pipeline reduces 800 → ~40 candidates  ·  VLM judges the 40 → final 20 + labels them

The seam is the whole design. The pipeline does the heavy reduction the VLM literally can't (context window). The VLM does the semantic call on the small survivor set, where its cost is bounded and its strength shows. Metadata the VLM never sees — that two shots are 40 seconds and 5 metres apart — is exactly what your time/geo fusion feeds the reduction. The two halves make each other better.

The one honest gap. That 77% is disagreement, not "VLM is better." Nothing tells us which pick is right yet — that needs the human judging pass EXP-2 already built the harness for. It is the single decision-critical unknown, and it's ~90% done.

What the literature says

The pipeline path is the textbook method, not a legacy hack. Personal-album summarization has, for 15+ years, meant: cluster into events by time + content, then pick a representative per event (affinity-propagation exemplars, aesthetic/sentiment ranking, multi-space face+background+time clustering). Your pipeline is this literature.

VLM-for-selection is a live frontier, not a settled win. 2024–25 work applies VLMs to image aesthetics & personalized quality (and it's improving), but surveys explicitly flag that a systematic "does a VLM actually beat a learned score at keeper selection" comparison is still missing. So finishing EXP-2's judging doesn't just settle your product — it's on the open edge of the field.

How to proceed

  1. Keep the pipeline as the structure engine. It's doing the part VLMs can't. Your scene-fusion work strengthens exactly this — better reduction = better candidate sets.
  2. Insert a VLM only at the two judgment points, on the reduced set: best-of-N within a cluster, and theme/caption/narrative. A cheaper VLM is fine for annotation, as you said.
  3. Finish EXP-2's human judging — this gates everything. Blind-pick keepers for the 13 clusters. VLM wins → route selection through it. Wash → keep the cheap score and save the money. This is the decision, and the harness already exists.
  4. Feed the VLM what it can't see. Time/geo priors sharpen the pipeline's reduction, so the VLM's small-set job gets easier and cheaper. That's the spec-103 work paying off twice.
Bottom line. Combination, with a clear seam: pipeline reduces & structures; VLM judges & narrates the survivors. Don't throw away the pipeline — it's the part the VLM can't do and the part the literature keeps re-deriving. The only open question is whether the VLM earns a seat at selection or just at annotation, and one afternoon of blind judging answers it.
