Defect scoring #3: Over/under-exposure

Goal. Benchmark the pipeline's exposure score (RuleBasedQuality._compute_exposure_quality: histogram-clipping penalty + entropy, 30% of the overall quality score) against human judgments, a trivial classical baseline, and deep IQA metrics.

Method

  • Dataset: PIQA23 Exposure attribute (Portrait IQA, CVPR 2023) — smartphone shots of the same scenes across an exposure ladder, human-scored via pairwise comparisons into JOD (just-objectionable-difference) units. Higher JOD = better. Local copy: D:\DataSets\PIQA23\Exposure + Scores_Exposure.csv.
  • Sample: 50 scenes, stratified by JOD to 30 images/scene = 1,500 images (of 5,116).
  • Ours: _compute_exposure_quality — (1 − clip0/255)·0.5 + entropy·0.5.
  • Classical: mid-gray distance — 1 − 2·|mean/255 − 0.5|. One line of code.
  • SOTA: MUSIQ and CLIPIQA (pyiqa, CPU, native resolution).
  • Metric: Spearman rank correlation (SROCC) vs human JOD — global and per-scene mean (per-scene is the fair test: JOD comparisons were collected within scene).
  • Reproduce: .venv/Scripts/python scripts/experiment_defectscore_exposure.py. Raw scores: data/scores.csv.

Why ours is weak — score compression. Human JOD spans 11.4 units (−6.7 … +4.7), but our exposure score packs 90% of all 1,500 images into [0.89, 0.99]: the entropy term is ≈1 for any natural photo, and the clipping penalty counts only pure 0/255 histogram bins, which modern smartphone pipelines (tone-mapping, denoise) almost never emit even in badly over/under-exposed shots. The metric ends up ranking on noise-level differences of a near-constant. A severely under-exposed image (JOD −3.0) scores 0.965 — above many well-exposed ones. Even the one-line mid-gray baseline beats it. MUSIQ is clearly better but far from the human ceiling; per-scene it never goes anti-correlated (min 0.05), unlike all the shallow metrics.

Verdict

Our exposure score barely discriminates (SROCC 0.28) and loses to a one-line brightness baseline (0.33); MUSIQ doubles it (0.57). The 30%-weighted exposure component of the overall quality score is close to a constant on real photos. Proposed directions (needs a spec, not implemented): (1) soft-clipping penalty — count near-black/near-white mass (e.g. <5 / >250) instead of exact 0/255 bins; (2) add the mid-gray term; (3) or replace the component with MUSIQ where its ~1s/image CPU cost is acceptable. PIQA23 also ships Details and Overall attribute scores — usable to validate sharpness against human perception later.
