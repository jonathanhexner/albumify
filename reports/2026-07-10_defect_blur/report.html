Defect scoring #1: Blur

Goal. Can the pipeline's sharpness score (Laplacian variance, sim_bench/quality_assessment/rule_based.py) detect real camera-shake blur, compared to a classical alternative and a state-of-the-art deep metric?

Method

  • Dataset: RealBlur-J (Rim et al., ECCV 2020) — 702 paired (blurred, tripod-sharp) photos of the same scene. Local copy: D:\occlusion_dataset\realblur\j_sample\{blur,gt}.
  • Ours: Laplacian variance (pipeline sharpness) + its production /1000-clip normalization.
  • Classical: Tenengrad (Sobel gradient energy above mean threshold, resolution-normalized).
  • SOTA: MUSIQ (multi-scale image quality transformer, pyiqa, CPU).
  • Metrics: pair accuracy = fraction of pairs where the sharp image outscores its blurred twin (scale-free, the album use-case: "pick the better shot"); ROC-AUC = separability of the two populations with a single global threshold (the "flag this image as blurry" use-case).
  • Reproduce: .venv/Scripts/python scripts/experiment_defectscore_blur.py. Raw scores: data/scores.csv.

Verdict

Our Laplacian sharpness is competitive with SOTA for blur. It wins within-scene ranking (99.4%, the best-shot-selection use-case) and only trails MUSIQ on absolute thresholding (AUC 0.871 vs 0.893 — Laplacian variance is content-dependent, so one global "blurry" threshold misfires on low-texture scenes). The 4 failed pairs (0.6%) are dark, noisy frames where sensor noise masquerades as sharpness — the same mechanism that breaks this metric completely on the noise benchmark (see companion report 2026-07-10_defect_noise). No change needed for blur ranking; for absolute flagging, MUSIQ or a content-normalized threshold would be better.
