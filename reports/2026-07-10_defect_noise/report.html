Defect scoring #2: Noise

Goal. The pipeline declares a noise metric in rule_based.py's docstring but never implemented one. Does any existing pipeline score detect real sensor noise — and what would a noise metric cost?

Method

  • Dataset: SIDD-Small sRGB (Abdelhamed et al., CVPR 2018) — 160 real smartphone scenes (5 phone models, ISO 50–10000), paired noisy capture / clean ground truth, pre-cropped patches. Local copy: D:\DataSets\SIDD_Small_sRGB_Only. Sampled 3 patch pairs per scene = 480 pairs.
  • Ours (proxies): overall rule-based quality score, and the Laplacian sharpness component alone. Hypothesis going in: noise inflates Laplacian variance, so noisy images would score as sharper.
  • Classical: wavelet noise-sigma estimate (skimage.restoration.estimate_sigma), negated so higher = cleaner.
  • SOTA: MUSIQ (pyiqa).
  • Metric: pair accuracy = fraction of pairs where the clean image outscores its noisy twin. Chance = 50%.
  • Reproduce: .venv/Scripts/python scripts/experiment_defectscore_noise.py. Raw scores: data/scores.csv.

The hypothesis is confirmed, catastrophically. Our scores are not merely blind to noise — they are inverted. Sensor noise is high-frequency energy, so Laplacian variance explodes on noisy images (median 12.5 clean → 487.9 noisy — a 39× inflation), and since sharpness carries 40% of the overall score, the noisy image wins every single one of the 480 pairs. At ISO 3200 a noisy patch scored overall 0.88 vs 0.32 for its clean twin. MUSIQ is also unreliable on these patches (61.9%, barely above chance — it was trained on whole photos, not 256-px crops). The 30-year-old wavelet estimator is essentially perfect and costs ~20 ms/image.

Verdict

The pipeline actively prefers noisy images. Any album containing high-ISO shots (night, indoor) will have its "best shot" selection biased toward the noisiest frame of a burst, and the blur gate is silently defeated by noise (a noisy blurred photo can outscore a clean sharp one). Recommended fix (proposal only, per workflow — needs a spec): (1) add estimate_sigma as a noise metric — near-perfect here and ~20 ms; (2) noise-correct the sharpness score, e.g. median-denoise before the Laplacian or subtract a sigma-dependent term; (3) re-run the RealBlur benchmark to confirm blur ranking survives the correction.
