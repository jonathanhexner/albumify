How the method works

A crooked photo has no metadata saying so — EXIF only records 90° steps. GeoCalib recovers the roll from the picture itself, but unlike edge/line tricks it reads meaning: it knows people stand up, walls are vertical, horizons are level. Worked example below on a high-tilt shot (20250822_123528, roll +16.5°):

1. Input
A photo taken with a tilted camera. The scene content is rotated.
2. Predict "up" everywhere
A neural net outputs, at every pixel, the direction gravity points — the green arrows. It infers this from semantic cues, so a slanted painting doesn't fool it the way a line detector would.
3. Measure the roll
All the arrows agree on one "scene up" (green). The angle between that and the screen's true vertical (white dashed) is the roll. A geometric optimiser fits the single camera roll that best explains the whole field.
4. Straighten
Rotate the image by the negative of that roll and the horizon levels out.
